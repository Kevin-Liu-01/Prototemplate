## Corrections to BRIEF.md found in the archives

1. **Kircher plate rights and edition.** The Commons file `Ricci_Guangqi_2.jpg` is licensed **CC BY-SA 3.0**, not public domain. It is a retouched scan (lines darkened, background cleaned, by the Commons user SteveMcCluskey). The scan comes from the **1670 French edition**, *La Chine d'Athanase Kirchere ... illustré* (Amsterdam, 1670), plate facing p. 201, not from the 1667 Latin edition. The unretouched master at Villanova is larger (4395 × 6223 against 3338 × 4988). Villanova attaches its standard rights notice and claims no license, and the 1670 engraving is public domain. Use the Villanova file to keep the plate free of the share-alike terms.
2. **Xu's cartouche** holds 徐 光啟, then 保祿 in a narrower column beside 光啟, then 玄扈 (his literary name), romanized by the engraver *Siü, quam, pao, lŏ, hiven, hú*. "徐光啟保祿" covers part of it. Ricci's cartouche reads 利瑪竇 西泰 (*Ly mà teú sī tāi*). The European engraver cut several characters wrongly. The Commons annotations list them: 利 has 丷 for 丨, 西 lacks a stroke, 徐 lacks 一, and 扈 is altered. Set the names in type beside the crop. Do not present the engraved characters as correct Chinese.
3. **LOC wdl_18198 is identified** (fact-check item 20): Euclid, *Elementa*, Campanus's Latin version, **Venice: Erhard Ratdolt, 25 May 1482**. This is the first printed edition. The copy is at the Bavarian State Library, shelfmark Rar. 292. It has **no margin references**: the margins carry the figures, and the text cites in line ("per secundam petitionem"). For arrows running from margin references back up the page, use Clavius 1574 fol. 21v–22r or Clavius 1591 p. 20. The 1591 page keys its margin references to superscript letters in the text (a 3. petit., b 1. petit., c 20. definit., d 15. definit., e 1. pronun.).
4. **Internet Archive rights** (fact-check item 22, IA half): the Clavius item has **no rights field**. Its metadata carries no `rights`, `licenseurl` or `possible-copyright-status` key. The book is from 1574 and is public domain by age. The ETH 1591 printing carries **PDM 1.0** in its MODS record.
5. **"definitio" is printed in the 1574 Clavius.** Fol. 1r has the heading DEFINITIONES and, in the commentary at its foot, "Quæ quidem definitio planius ac facilius". The citation key on fol. 21r reads "1. def. · Prima definitio", "1. pet. · Prima petitio", "1. pron. · Primum pronunciatum, seu axioma". These are Clavius's three Latin terms behind 界說, 求作 and 公論.
6. **Q.E.F. wording.** Clavius ends Prop. I.1 with "Quod faciendum erat." (fol. 22r; also 1591 p. 20). The vocabulary table writes *quod erat faciendum*. If the film shows the Latin, quote it as Clavius printed it.
7. **Clavius's Prop. I.1 figure is lettered A B C D**, with D at the lower crossing of the circles. The heading reads "PROBLEMA I. PROPOSITIO I." and the statement "Super data recta linea terminata triangulum AEquilaterum constituere." The 1482 Ratdolt figure uses lowercase a, b, c, d, f, h.
8. **"Geometria" on the pages.** The 1574 title page has no *geometria*, which fits the post. But Clavius's running heads read "EVCLID. GEOM." (1574) and "EVCLIDIS GEOMETRIÆ" (1591), and Ratdolt's opening rubric says "in artem geometriam". In the 幾何 beat, show the title page, or crop the running head off.
9. **The 1574 title page appears to be a woodcut frame, not an engraving.** It has an architectural border with IHS, a terrestrial globe and an armillary sphere. A handwritten ownership note runs across the title text.

## Fonts (`fonts/`)

All OFL 1.1. Neither license names a Reserved Font Name. Use `fonts/fonts.css`, which declares private family names (`JY Han Subset`, `JY Han Full`, `JY Latin`) and the variables `--jy-han`, `--jy-latin` and `--jy-mixed`. A composition should never write `'EB Garamond'` or `'Noto Serif TC'`: per MOTION.md, a literal Google Fonts family name makes the compiler fetch it from Google Fonts.

## Globe data

`assets/places.json` holds every coordinate in BRIEF.md's globe section, copied as given: Rome (Collegio Romano), Macau, Shaozhou, Beijing (Jesuit residence near Xuanwumen), Xujiahui (Xu Guangqi's tomb, Guangqi Park), and the optional Alexandria. Each place carries the script's caption string and every date the post gives for it, quoted. Dates taken from the script or the fact-check list are marked with that source. The contested dates (Shaozhou c. 1590–91; Beijing "translated 1606–07, printed 1607") are flagged. The `name_zh` labels are standard place names that BRIEF.md does not give.
