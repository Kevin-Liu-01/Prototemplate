## How it is built

Run in this order from this folder; each step reads the files the one before wrote.

1. `tools/lines.json`: every spoken line of `SCRIPT.md` (the text given to the voice, with numbers written as said), with `--prev` and `--next` for each. `node tools/record.mjs <ids>` records a take through `kit/audio/el.mjs line` (the only reader of the ElevenLabs key) and transcribes it with `el.mjs hear`, into `audio/<id>.mp3`, `.json` (the take's own character timings) and `.stt.json`. `tools/hearctx.sh <id>` transcribes a short Mandarin take behind a known Mandarin carrier phrase. `python3 tools/ledger.py` prints the ledger below.
2. `python3 tools/splice.py`: the Mandarin takes joined from phrases of several Yun takes (`zh1s`, `zh3s`).
3. `python3 tools/tune.py`: the tone-corrected Mandarin takes (`zh4t`, `zh5t`, `zh6t`, `zh7t`): Yun's own takes with single syllables re-pitched by `tools/psola.py` (below). `python3 tools/pitch.py <take>` is the check: each syllable's F0 with a 70 Hz floor, in semitones.
4. `python3 tools/timeline.py`: places every take by its speech start, reads the cut words and the Mandarin characters from the takes' alignments, plans the beats and the subtitle bar under `SCRIPT.md`'s reading floor, and writes `audio/cues.json` and `lib/cues.js`.
5. The composition: `index.html` loads `fonts/fonts.css`, `lib/page.css`, `kit/gsap.min.js`, `lib/page.js` (the treatment's stage: scans as canvas planes in their native pixels, the camera, the lamp, the brush strokes, plus marks boxes and canvas layers), `lib/data.js` (the measured page coordinates, the treatment's plus the film's), `lib/cues.js`, `lib/liuhui.js` (beat 6), `lib/stems.js` (beat 9), `lib/end.js` (beat 11) and `lib/film.js` (the shots, the bars, the grain, `render`). The inline script at the end of the body builds the film and registers one paused timeline on `window.__timelines.main`.
5a. Beat 6's plan comes from `tools/beat6/` (Python and numpy). `geo.py` holds the print's points, the copies (quarter turns of the print: ◣ as printed, ◤ a quarter clockwise, ◥ a half, ◢ a quarter counter-clockwise) and the strip with its slots. `sim.py` reproduces exactly the pose `lib/liuhui.js` draws (path, ease, turn window, pivot, the fit from the print to the exact figure, the parting, which round 8 sets to 0). `demo.py` is the planner (round 7's idea, round 8's pair): `plan.json` holds the copies' places (`layout`), `part` (0), the demonstration (`demo`: its time, the slot rectangle's kind, its two halves with their roles in the slot rectangle, path bends `a1 n1 a2 n2` and turn windows `rs re`; each turns about its centroid), `sources` (which halves make every other rectangle), the print schedule (`print`: first time, gap, reveal length, the light's rise, hold and go, how far it lightens a source, the print's ghost `ghostC`) and the measures; `RECTS` and `ORDER` in `demo.py` hold the slots and the reading order. `python3 demo.py scan` tries every pair of 朱 halves that can close the first rectangle with a turn of at most a quarter, on a half-cell grid of places for their two copies, and ranks them (Round 8). `python3 demo.py optimise plan.json out.json <iters> <seed> [fine]` walks both halves' bends and turn windows against the eighteen resting pieces and each other (paper of the same colour, of another colour, each other, past the slot, path, corner travel, peak speed and turn rate), and `python3 demo.py check plan.json` measures at 60 fps. `report.py` prints the layout with its clearances in the frame, the demonstration's overlaps by pair, the centroid paths, chords and farthest-corner travel, speeds and turns, the print schedule with each rectangle's sources and the light's timing, the hold, the stretch and the slots measured against the strip. `python3 tools/beat6/embed.py tools/beat6/plan.json` fits the end framing (round 4's), checks that every copy lies inside the window and clear of the strip, and writes the plan into `lib/liuhui.js` as its `PLAN` constant; nothing at render time reads Python's output. ### Tone correction (round 2)

The model reads some syllables in the wrong tone in every take, whatever the text says. With a pitch track floored at 70 Hz (`tools/pitch.py`; the round-1 `tools/tones.py` floors at 140 Hz, so a low tone clipped there and read like a fall), Yun's 界 is low and level in all twelve takes that hold it (`zh4` to `zh4l`, the note takes `zh5` to `zh5i`): 界說 as written, 介說, 戒說, pinyin, 世界說, 邊界說, 界線, the heading 界說三十六則, a single 界. That is jiě, so 界說 is heard as 解說, "explanation", which is the word the note sets against it. Her 丁 and 何 fall at the end of a phrase in every take (dìng, hè), and in the pinyin Stems take 甲 jumps from 272 Hz to 142 Hz in the middle of the syllable, so it sounds like two syllables.

`tools/psola.py` re-pitches one syllable at a time, TD-PSOLA in numpy: it marks each glottal period of her own voice, then lays those periods out again at the spacing the target pitch needs, so the formants (her timbre) do not move and the samples around the syllable are untouched. `tools/tune.py` holds the target contours, written in semitones against 217 Hz (her speech median) and modelled on her own clean tones in the same takes: a fourth tone such as 故 starts near 310 Hz and falls to about 150, a first tone such as 說 sits level near 250.

## Music and sound

- **Music.** Two Music API compositions, the most allowed, from `CONCEPT.md`'s prompt (cello, felt piano, bowed double bass; no guqin, no pentatonic figures, no gong). Take 1 (chosen) came back 118.0 s long for 100 s asked: a D minor piece (pitch classes F, D, A, B♭, C, G; the B♭ a semitone off A, so no five-note set), cello and felt piano throughout, its last piano note at music time 104.80 s. Take 2 came back exactly 100 s but opens with 10 s of silence and stops at 92 s. `el.mjs hear` returned "[on-hold music]" and "[outro jingle]": no voice in either. The film plays take 1 so that its last piano note lands on the cut to the end card: music time = film time + (104.80 - 96.43) = film time + 8.37 (`tools/mix.py` computes the offset from the beat 11 cut). The master: a 35 Hz high-pass, a 3 dB low shelf at 110 Hz, dips of 2 dB at 1.1 kHz and 3 dB at 2.6 kHz to leave the voice its band, a 1.2 s fade in and a 0.8 s fade from 99.2 s.
- **Ride (round 2).** The composition itself runs from about -43 to -12 dBFS, so a fixed duck left it as loud as the dialogue in its swells (the 齟齬 hold, the Stems). ## Traps

- **GSAP timelines are thenables.** A Playwright `waitForFunction(() => window.__timelines.main)` resolves the timeline as a promise and waits for a paused timeline to finish, forever. Return a boolean (`!!window.__timelines.main`).
- **Build from the inline script.** With the film's builder as a self-running external script, `hyperframes check` ran it before `#f` existed. `lib/film.js` defines `JYBuildFilm`, and the inline script at the end of the body calls it and registers the timeline.
- **A clip with static children** draws the lint warning `nested_structure_needs_subcomposition`; the frame `#f` is the clip itself and its children are made at build time.
- **Frames for review are picked by index.** `ffmpeg -ss T -i` before the input landed one frame late at the cuts; `select='eq(n,N)'` shows the frames on both sides of a cut exactly.
- **Colour-dodge inside a crowded plane** rendered as plain compositing. `screen` does composite there (the beat 6 hairline).
- **Bare paper.** A high-pass "grain" taken from a patch with text in it embosses that text into the rebuilt paper. Use seeded noise at the paper's own amplitude, or, on a bitonal scan, the scan's own paper colour, which is one flat value.
- **Fonts.** No literal family name in the CSS: every face comes through `fonts/fonts.css`'s variables. The render log has no Google Fonts line.
- **The renderer's audio ceiling.** HyperFrames 0.8.106 turns the whole AAC track down to -1.5 dBTP when its true peak passes -1 dBTP, and its limiter worklet has no lookahead. All peak control happens in the masters; the offline sum and the render's audio are compared after every render.
- **The Music API** returns its own length (118 s for 100 s asked) and its own form; edit it to the film rather than prompting for timing. Its dynamics are its own too: ride it level before ducking it.
- **Take tails.** Many takes end with a 40 to 60 ms blip of the `--next` text. `tools/timeline.py` measures the speech end and trims each clip there (plus 0.12 s).
- **The recogniser on classical Chinese** prefers common words (匕首 for 筆受, 解说 for 界說, 集合 for 幾何) and loses its language on two- to four-syllable clips; `tools/hearctx.sh` puts a known Mandarin phrase in front. It cannot tell a tone by itself; a pitch track can.
- **Pitch tracks need a low floor.** A YIN floor of 140 Hz clips Yun's third tone (down to about 120 Hz) and makes a low syllable read as a fall. `tools/pitch.py` floors at 70 Hz.
- **The TTS's tones are not the text's.** Yun reads 界 low in every spelling and every context, and lets a phrase-final first or second tone fall. Re-pitching her own periods (`tools/psola.py`) fixes a syllable without changing her voice; respelling and context did not.
- **Polyphones in the TTS.** Yun read 論 as lún until the syllable was written as pinyin (lùn) inside the line.
- **Persisting state across shots.** A plane keeps every mark the reader made on it, which is the point on the 1607 page. Anything else a shot changes on a shared plane (a lifted slip, a bare-paper patch, a shadow, a transform) must be put back in that shot's `leave` hook, or a worker that played through the shot draws a different frame from one that seeked past it. Check with `tools/shoot.mjs --fresh` against a sequential shot.
- **GPU canvases are not deterministic under load.** In round 2 two draft renders differed in 1470 of 3000 frames. Two lossless renders (`--crf 0 --fps 10`) found the input frames: one worker printed the 1607 page (p008) a few code values differently through all its frames of beats 4 and 8, in one run and not the other. Whether Chrome puts a large 2D canvas on the GPU depends on its memory budget at that moment, and a GPU canvas paints a scan slightly differently.
