# Modern Hebrew מִלּוֹן

100.0 s · 100 s cut · Narrator: Clara · Reader: Tomer

Eliezer Ben-Yehuda built new Hebrew words from old roots, and his dictionary of 1908 marks his accepted coinages, such as the word for bicycle, with a small sign, prints a different sign on electricity, leaves out Pines’s word for the tomato, which is the word Hebrew speakers use today, and names among its funders a society that was building a school in Haifa, whose board chose German for the sciences in 1913, nine years before the Mandate for Palestine named English, Arabic and Hebrew as the country’s official languages.
