kb-translate-react-app: credits and sources
How do I translate a React app? One of General Translation's product films (formerly the
knowledge base series, film 10), for its React page. The end card points to the docs page,
generaltranslation.com/docs/react/react-spa-quickstart.
Film: the story cut, 48.97 s, 1920 x 1080, 60 fps. Built from 2026-10-12; built again 2026-10-13 for
the product pages, with an answer that names General Translation; and again that evening, with the
answer alone on its card and npx said as one word; and again 2026-10-14, with the key words of each
heading and of the answer in the blue accent, and JSON said "j-sawn". It replaces the recited cut
(v1, 44.62 s, 2026-10-10).

SCRIPT
- From General Translation's docs page "React SPA Quickstart" (generaltranslation/content
  docs/en-US/react/react-spa-quickstart.mdx, main 261320d), with the loader from
  docs/en-US/react/guides/spa/configuring-vite-spa.mdx. The film covers single-page React apps
  (Vite); server-rendered and React Router apps have their own quickstarts. It tells the quickstart
  as a story: someone in Paris gets the app in English although the browser asks for French,
  because its only text is English and the app loads no other language. The answer on the question
  card is the quickstart's steps in one sentence with General Translation as its subject: "General
  Translation lets you initialize gt-react before the app renders and wrap its text in T." The
  steps are the quickstart's: gt.config.json and a loadTranslations file, an entry file that calls
  initializeGTSPA before the rest of the app loads, <T>, and sign in, the project ID and npx gt
  translate; the entry file imports gt-react, and its install stays on the docs page. Paris is a
  sample place; the film says nothing about anyone there. The code, commands and file names are
  checked against the docs and the open-source code (generaltranslation/gt: gt-react and
  @generaltranslation/react-core 11.4.11, gt 2.26.1). BRIEF.md cites each one. The research brief
  is by the knowledge base series' research lane (BRIEF.md, sections 1 to 8).

CODE ON SCREEN
- src/components/Welcome.tsx before and after <T>, gt.config.json, src/index.ts, index.html, the
  terminal commands and .env.local: the quickstart's code; src/loadTranslations.ts: the Vite
  guide's. Long lines are in Prettier's wrapped form, two trailing comments and one blank row are
  left out, and src/index.ts and index.html are set at 30 px so each file fits the panel, as
  BRIEF.md section 9 says.
- GT_PROJECT_ID uses the docs' placeholder, your-project-id. No key is shown.

THE APP PAGE
- The page's English strings are the quickstart's: Welcome to my app, This content will be
  translated automatically. The Spanish, French and Japanese strings are drafts written for this
  film and film 9; they are not output of General Translation's API, and they wait for a native
  speaker's review. kb.json (scene.screen.blocks) holds every one of them.
- The language code over the page's corner (fr, then es, ja and fr) is the language each visitor's
  browser asks for, which gt-react reads before the app renders. The page has no language menu.

MARKS
- The doubled-line GT mark (kit/brand/gt-mark.svg), small in the lower left corner of the answer
  card, and the General Translation lockup, the bar monogram over the name
  (kit/marks/bar-monogram-lockup.svg), on the end card.

TYPE
- Inter (rsms/inter, SIL Open Font License 1.1), kit/fonts/InterVariable.woff2, through var(--font).

- Japanese is set in the machine's Hiragino Sans, which kit/kb/kb-story.js names by the text's
  language; no face is fetched.

VOICE
- Narrator: Frederick Surrey, an ElevenLabs library voice (eleven_multilingual_v2, stability 0.55,
  style 0.2, speed 1.0; kit/audio/voice.json). Eight takes, one per line, through kit/kb/take.mjs
  and kit/audio/el.mjs: five recorded 2026-10-12, the answer's recorded 2026-10-13 with the question
  sent before it so it reads as a reply, and two recorded 2026-10-10 for the recited cut, whose
  lines are the same word for word. The translate line, which says npx, was recorded again
  2026-10-13 evening, with npx said as one word, in place of its take of 2026-10-10, and the config
  line, which says JSON, was recorded again 2026-10-14, with JSON said "j-sawn", in place of its
  take of 2026-10-12. None is slowed, sped or stretched.
