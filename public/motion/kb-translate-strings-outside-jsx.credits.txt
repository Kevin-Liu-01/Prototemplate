kb-translate-strings-outside-jsx: credits and sources
How do I translate strings outside JSX? One of General Translation's product films (formerly
the knowledge base series, film 11), for its Next.js page. The end card points to the docs page,
generaltranslation.com/docs/react/guides/translating-strings.
Film: the story cut, 49.15 s, 1920 x 1080, 60 fps. Built from 2026-10-12; built again 2026-10-13 for
the product pages, with an answer that names General Translation; and again that evening, with the
answer alone on its card and npx said as one word; and again 2026-10-14, with the key words of each
heading and of the answer in the blue accent. It replaces the recited cut (v1, 43.88 s, 2026-10-10).

SCRIPT
- From General Translation's docs guide "Translating strings" (generaltranslation/content
  docs/en-US/react/guides/translating-strings.mdx, main 261320d), with the references for useGT,
  getGT, msg and useMessages, the Next.js quickstart and the gt init and gt translate command
  pages. The film covers gt-next; the guide's other tabs cover gt-react, gt-tanstack-start and
  gt-react-native. It tells the guide as a story: someone in Vienna opens a product list whose
  search placeholder and status badges are in English, because the placeholder is an attribute and
  the badges come from a constant. The answer on the answer card is the guide's answer shortened
  to one sentence with General Translation as its subject: "General Translation translates
  placeholders with useGT and constants with msg and useMessages." Its third part, getGT in async
  components, is a step. The steps are the guide's: useGT for the placeholder, getGT in an async
  component, msg with useMessages for the constant, and npx gt translate, in an app already set up
  with npx gt init. Vienna is a sample place; the film says nothing about anyone there. The code,
  commands and file names are checked against the docs and the open-source code
  (generaltranslation/gt: gt-next and @generaltranslation/react-core 11.4.11, gt 2.26.1). BRIEF.md
  cites each one. The research brief is by the knowledge base series' research lane (BRIEF.md,
  sections 1 to 8).

CODE ON SCREEN
- Search.tsx and Badge.tsx: the guide's Next.js code, with long lines on more rows (whitespace
  only) so each fits the panel, and a before state with the strings as plain literals.
  gt.config.json: the quickstart's form, with this film's locales. The project's files are those
  npx gt init leaves in an App Router app. The terminal command is the docs'. BRIEF.md section 9
  lists each change.

THE APP PAGE
- The page's English strings are the guide's: Search products, Active, Paused. The Spanish,
  Japanese and German strings are drafts written for this film; they are not output of General
  Translation's API, and they wait for a native speaker's review. kb.json
  (scene.screen.blocks) holds every one of them.
- The language code over the page's corner (de, then es, ja and de) is the language the visitor's
  browser asks for, which gt-next reads. The page has no language menu.
- The magnifying glass is Heroicons 2.2.0 20 solid magnifying-glass (MIT, Tailwind Labs).

MARKS
- The doubled-line GT mark (kit/brand/gt-mark.svg), small in the lower left corner of the answer
  card, and the same mark, larger, on the end card.

TYPE
- Inter (rsms/inter, SIL Open Font License 1.1), kit/fonts/InterVariable.woff2, through var(--font).
- Geist Mono 1.701 (vercel/geist-font, SIL Open Font License 1.1, kit/kb/fonts/OFL.txt) for code.
- Japanese is set in the machine's Hiragino Sans, which kit/kb/kb-story.js names by the text's
  language; no face is fetched.

VOICE
- Narrator: Frederick Surrey, an ElevenLabs library voice (eleven_multilingual_v2, stability 0.55,
  style 0.2, speed 1.0; kit/audio/voice.json). Eight takes, one per line, through kit/kb/take.mjs
  and kit/audio/el.mjs: six recorded 2026-10-12 (one of them recorded three times), the answer's
  recorded 2026-10-13 with the question sent before it so it reads as a reply, and one recorded
  2026-10-10 for the recited cut, whose line is the same word for word. The translate line, which
  says npx, was recorded again that evening, with npx said as one word. None is slowed, sped or
  stretched.

MUSIC
- The knowledge base series bed: composed for the series by the ElevenLabs Music API (music_v1,
  instrumental, 60 s, 2026-10-09; kit/kb/audio/series-bed.prompt.txt), cut to this film on its own
  bar lines by kit/kb/mix.py so its held chord falls on the end card.

  kit/kb/kb-listpage.js) for the product list.
