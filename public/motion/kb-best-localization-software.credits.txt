kb-best-localization-software: credits and sources
What is the best localization software for developers? One of General Translation's product
films (formerly the knowledge base series, film 8), for its Platform page. The end card links the
docs page generaltranslation.com/docs/overview/get-started.
Film: the story cut, 43.78 s, 1920 x 1080, 60 fps. Built from 2026-10-12; built again 2026-10-13 for
the product pages, with an answer that names General Translation and the docs link, and line 4
without its opening "With General Translation"; and again that evening, with the answer alone on its
card and npx said as one word; and again 2026-10-14, with the key words of each heading and of the
answer in the blue accent. It replaces the recited cut (v1, 42.38 s, 2026-10-10).

SCRIPT
- From the knowledge base article "What is the best localization software for developers?" by
  Jackie Chen (2026-10-09), generaltranslation/content kb/best-localization-software.mdx. The film
  tells it as a story: someone in Mexico City gets the article's Home page in English, because its
  English is in the component and nothing in the project translates it. The answer on the answer
  card is the article's summary shortened to one sentence (its criterion, without the "instead of"
  clause), with General Translation as its subject: "General Translation covers the whole pipeline,
  from your code to translation and delivery." The steps are General Translation's own pipeline
  from the article and the docs (generaltranslation/content docs/en-US: the gt init and gt
  translate pages, the Next.js quickstart, the Locadex quickstart and automation pages): npx gt
  init, the content in a <T> component, one command in the build, and Locadex's pull requests once
  the repository is connected. Mexico City is a sample place; the film says nothing about anyone
  there. The code, commands and file names are checked against the docs and the open-source code
  (generaltranslation/gt main 1e3cc41: gt-next 11.4.11, gt 2.26.1). It names no other company,
  states no speed or price, and makes no claim about another product. BRIEF.md cites each line.

CODE ON SCREEN
- app/page.tsx: the article's example, with its paragraph set over two rows (JSX reads it as the
  same text), first with a fragment where <T> goes and then with its content in <T>.
- The project's files: create-next-app's App Router files (vercel/next.js), then with
  gt.config.json, and the repository with public/_gt/ and its three files and the
  loadTranslations.js that npx gt init writes at the project root.
- package.json: the Next.js quickstart's build script; "next build" is create-next-app's
  default. public/_gt/ and its files: the output path npx gt init sets (the quickstart's
  files.gt.output) for the film's locales, de, ja and es.

THE APP PAGE AND THE PULL REQUEST
- The page's English strings are the article's: Welcome to our app, This content is translated
  automatically. The German, Japanese and Spanish strings were written for this film as drafts;
  they are not output of General Translation's API, and they wait for a native speaker's review.
  kb.json (scene.screen.blocks) holds every one of them.
- The language code over the page's corner (es, then de, ja and es) is the language the visitor's
  browser asks for, which gt-next reads. The page has no language menu.
- The pull request is a generic drawing (bars, a branch line, the changed files); it shows no
  other company's interface, and no title, number, author or branch name.

VOICE
