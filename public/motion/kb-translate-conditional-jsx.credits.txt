kb-translate-conditional-jsx: credits and sources
How do I translate conditional text in JSX? One of General Translation's product films (formerly
the knowledge base series, film 6), for its React page. The end card links the docs page
generaltranslation.com/docs/react/guides/handling-plurals-and-branches.
Film: the story cut, 46.08 s, 1920 x 1080, 60 fps. Built 2026-10-12; built again 2026-10-13 for the
product pages, with an answer that names General Translation and the docs link; and again that
evening, with the answer alone on its card and npx said as one word; and again 2026-10-14, with the
key words of each heading and of the answer in the blue accent. It replaces the recited cuts (v1,
38.38 s, and v2, 40.62 s, both 2026-10-10).

SCRIPT
- From the knowledge base article "How do I translate conditional text in JSX?" by Ernest McCarter
  (2026-10-09), generaltranslation/content kb/translate-conditional-jsx.mdx. The film tells the
  article's case as a story: someone in Shanghai opens a page and its lunch sentence is in English,
  because its place, home or work, comes from a ternary of English words and nothing in the app
  translates the sentence. The answer on the answer card is the article's summary without its
  opening "Instead of" clause and its reason clause, with General Translation as its subject:
  "General Translation lets you wrap whole sentences in T and express conditions with Branch." The
  steps are the setup (npx gt init, with the languages and the sign-in that npx gt translate
  needs), the article's <T> with a <Branch>, and npx gt translate. Shanghai is a sample place; the
  film says nothing about anyone there. The code, commands and claims are checked against General
  Translation's docs (generaltranslation/content docs/en-US) and the open-source code
  (generaltranslation/gt main 1e3cc41: gt-react 11.4.11, gt 2.26.1). BRIEF.md and the script cite
  each one.

CODE ON SCREEN
- Lunch.jsx, before and after: the article's sentence (lines 59 to 61) with the ternary the
  article describes (lines 11 to 13 and 28), then with <T> and the article's <Branch>, its props on
  their own rows, and the import from gt-react.
- The terminal's npx gt init is the CLI's setup wizard (docs/en-US/cli/reference/commands/init.mdx);
  gt.config.json is the React quickstart's form with this film's locales, ja and zh.
- The Chinese translation is the article's (lines 78 to 82), labelled as the translation <T>
  renders, not code a developer writes.

THE APP PAGE
- The English sentence is the article's. The Chinese sentences for home and for work are the
  article's (lines 71 and 72). The Japanese sentence was written for this film and checked in a
  second pass. None of the page's translations is output of General Translation's API, and each
  waits for a native speaker's review, as does the reading that both languages put the place
  before the verb. kb.json (scene.screen.states) holds every one of them.
- The page has no language menu. The language code over its corner (zh, en, ja) is the language
  the visitor's browser asks for, which gt-react reads.
