Texts set in type

- The table of the coats of the eye: the Arabic names as Max Meyerhof prints
  them on Arabic p. 80 of The Book of the Ten Treatises on the Eye (1928),
  where the Greek names stand beside them in Arabic letters; the Greek names
  in Greek letters as Meyerhof's English translation gives them (p. 4), with
  their meanings; the Latin names from Constantine the African's Liber de
  oculis, in modern spelling.
- The people's names and dates: after Bergsträsser 1925, Meyerhof 1928,
  Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿa (ʿUyūn al-anbāʾ, ch. 8.29 to 8.31) and Ibn al-Qifṭī
  (Taʾrīkh al-ḥukamāʾ).
- "This is my habit in everything I translate.": Ḥunayn's sentence on
  Bergsträsser's Arabic p. 5, l. 7, translated for this film and checked
  against Bergsträsser's German translation (p. 4).
- D. W. Tschanz, "Hunayn bin Ishaq: The Great Translator", Journal of the
  International Society for the History of Islamic Medicine 1, no. 3 (2003),
  cited as an example of the popular account. Dimitri Gutas, Greek Thought,
  Arabic Culture (London: Routledge, 1998), pp. 53 to 60; M.-G.
  Balty-Guesdon (1992); M. Di Branco (2012): titles set in type only.
