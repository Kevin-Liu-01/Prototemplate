kb-add-i18n-to-nextjs: credits and sources
How do I add i18n to a Next.js app? One of General Translation's product films (formerly the
knowledge base series, film 9), for its Next.js page. The end card points to the docs page,
generaltranslation.com/docs/react/nextjs-quickstart.
Film: the story cut, 50.62 s, 1920 x 1080, 60 fps. Built from 2026-10-12; built again 2026-10-13 for
the product pages, with an answer that names General Translation; and again that evening, with the
answer alone on its card and npx said as one word; and again 2026-10-14, with the key words of each
heading and of the answer in the blue accent. It replaces the recited cut (v1, 43.70 s, 2026-10-10).

SCRIPT
- From General Translation's docs page "Next.js App Router Quickstart" (generaltranslation/content
  docs/en-US/react/nextjs-quickstart.mdx, main 261320d), with the delivery modes from
  docs/en-US/react/guides/storing-translations.mdx and docs/en-US/react/guides/configuring.mdx. The
  film tells it as a story: someone in Osaka who reads Japanese, on a browser that asks for
  English, gets the quickstart's page in English with no way to choose Japanese, because its text
  is plain JSX and the app has no i18n library. The answer on the answer card is the quickstart's
  steps in one sentence with General Translation as its subject: "General Translation lets you add
  i18n with gt-next: wrap text in T and generate translations." The steps are the quickstart's:
  withGTConfig, gt.config.json, a loadTranslations file, the GTProvider, <T> with a
  <LocaleSelector>, and npx gt translate before next build; the answer names gt-next, and its
  install and the production credentials stay on the docs page. Osaka is a sample place; the film
  says nothing about anyone there. The code, commands and file names are checked against the docs
  and the open-source code (generaltranslation/gt: gt-next and gt-react 11.4.11, gt 2.26.1).
  BRIEF.md cites each one. The research brief is by the knowledge base series' research lane
  (BRIEF.md, sections 1 to 8).

CODE ON SCREEN
- app/page.tsx before its steps, next.config.ts, gt.config.json, src/loadTranslations.ts,
  app/layout.tsx, app/page.tsx after them and package.json: the quickstart's code, with the long
  lines in Prettier's wrapped form and the blank row after two imports left out so each file fits
  the panel, as BRIEF.md section 9 says. "next build" is create-next-app's default
  (vercel/next.js).

THE APP PAGE
- The page's English strings are the quickstart's: Welcome to my app, This content will be
  translated automatically. The Spanish, French and Japanese strings are drafts written for this
  film and film 10; they are not output of General Translation's API, and they wait for a native
  speaker's review. kb.json (scene.screen.blocks) holds every one of them. The selector's labels
  are the ones <LocaleSelector> renders, each locale's own name. The chevron is Heroicons 2.2.0
  16 solid chevron-down (MIT, Tailwind Labs).
- The language code over the page's corner (en) is the language the visitor's browser asks for;
  once the page has its language menu, the menu shows the language instead.

MARKS
- The doubled-line GT mark (kit/brand/gt-mark.svg), small in the lower left corner of the answer
  card, and the same mark, larger, on the end card.

TYPE
- Inter (rsms/inter, SIL Open Font License 1.1), kit/fonts/InterVariable.woff2, through var(--font).
- Geist Mono 1.701 (vercel/geist-font, SIL Open Font License 1.1, kit/kb/fonts/OFL.txt) for code.
- Japanese is set in the machine's Hiragino Sans, which kit/kb/kb-story.js names by the text's
  language; no face is fetched.

VOICE
- Narrator: Frederick Surrey, an ElevenLabs library voice (eleven_multilingual_v2, stability 0.55,
  style 0.2, speed 1.0; kit/audio/voice.json). Seven takes, one per line, through kit/kb/take.mjs
  and kit/audio/el.mjs: six recorded 2026-10-12 (two of them recorded twice, one retake used), and
  the answer recorded again 2026-10-13 (four times, the third used), with the question sent before
  it so it reads as a reply. The build line, which says npx, was recorded again that evening, with
  npx said as one word. None is slowed, sped or stretched.

MUSIC
- The knowledge base series bed: composed for the series by the ElevenLabs Music API (music_v1,
  instrumental, 60 s, 2026-10-09; kit/kb/audio/series-bed.prompt.txt), cut to this film on its own
  bar lines by kit/kb/mix.py so its held chord falls on the end card.

DRAWING
- The drawing grammar of films/gif-how-gt-works (vector interface panels, doubled-line connectors,
  black and white with one blue accent), through motion/kit/kb, its story stage
  (kit/kb/kb-story.js) and its screen module (kit/kb/kb-screen.js) for the app page.
