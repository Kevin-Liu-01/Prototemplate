kb-i18n-without-translation-files: credits and sources
Can I do i18n without translation files? The first of General Translation's product films
(formerly the knowledge base series), for its Next.js page. The end card links the docs page
generaltranslation.com/docs/react/guides/translating-jsx.
Film: the story cut, 47.43 s, 1920 x 1080, 60 fps. Built 2026-10-12 and rendered again the same day
on the kit's second round (the question card's exit, the Japanese face); built again 2026-10-13 for
the product pages, with an answer that names General Translation and the docs link; and again that
evening, with the answer alone on its card and npx said as one word; and again 2026-10-14, with the
key words of each heading and of the answer in the blue accent, and JSON said "j-sawn". It replaces
the recited cuts (v1, 34.43 s, 2026-10-09; v2, 40.43 s, 2026-10-10).

SCRIPT
- From the knowledge base article "Can I do i18n without translation files?" by Jackie Chen and
  Ernest McCarter (2026-10-09), generaltranslation/content kb/i18n-without-translation-files.mdx.
  The film tells the article's own case as a story: someone in Tokyo opens a checkout page in
  Japanese and finds one line in English, because its key went into en.json and never reached
  ja.json (the article's line 13). The answer on the answer card is the article's summary in one
  sentence with General Translation as its subject: "General Translation lets you stop writing
  translation files with gt-react or gt-next." The steps are the article's: npx gt init (its
  "Getting started", in the docs' form), the English written inside a <T> component, and npx gt
  translate in the build script. Tokyo is a sample place; the film says nothing about anyone
  there. The code, commands and file names are checked against General Translation's docs
  (generaltranslation/content docs/en-US) and the open-source code (generaltranslation/gt main
  1e3cc41: gt-next 11.4.11, gt 2.26.1). BRIEF.md cites each one.

CODE ON SCREEN
- The outline of the project's files (./): en.json and ja.json with the keys each holds,
  summary.title, summary.total_label and summary.tax_notice (the article's key names), and an
  empty row where ja.json lacks summary.tax_notice. The article prints neither file, so the
  outline shows only which keys each file holds.
- gt.config.json: the Next.js quickstart's file (docs/en-US/react/nextjs-quickstart.mdx), with the
  settings npx gt init writes for these answers.
- CheckoutSummary.tsx, before and after: the article's examples, adapted as BRIEF.md says (the
  two lines with props are left out, and <T> wraps the <div> as in the article, with <T><div> and
  </div></T> on one line each).
- package.json and .gitignore: the article and the Next.js quickstart; "next build", /node_modules
  and /.next/ are create-next-app's defaults (vercel/next.js).
- The file panel: the four per-locale JSON files, gt.config.json, and public/_gt/ with es.json,
  fr.json and ja.json, the output of files.gt.output for the three locales.

THE APP PAGE
- A checkout page in the kit's drawing: a heading, two item rows drawn as bars, the total, the
  tax note and a button. Its English strings are the article's: Order Summary, Total (before
  tax):, Tax calculated at checkout. The Spanish, French and Japanese strings were written for this
  film and checked in a second pass; they are not output of General Translation's API, and they
  wait for a native speaker's review. kb.json (scene.screen.blocks) holds every one of them.
- The page has no language menu. The language code over its corner (ja, en, es, fr) is the
  language the visitor's browser asks for, which gt-next reads.

MARKS
- The doubled-line GT mark (kit/brand/gt-mark.svg), small in the lower left corner of the answer
  card, and the same mark, larger, on the end card.

VOICE

  2026-10-13, with the question sent before it so it reads as a reply, the two lines that say npx
  recorded again that evening, with npx said as one word, and the two lines that say JSON recorded
  again 2026-10-14, with JSON said "j-sawn". None is slowed, sped or stretched.
