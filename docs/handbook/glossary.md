# Glossary

This glossary defines the terms that appear in Kevin Liu's messages, in the General Translation (GT) skills and in the code of gt-cloud and Prototemplate. Each entry defines its term in one to three sentences and names the file, skill or document that owns it. The owner holds the full rule, so where the two disagree the owner is right and this page needs a fix. The vocabulary covers 2026-07-20 to 2026-10-05, and the retired terms in the last section point to [decisions.md](decisions.md).

Paths without a prefix are in a Prototemplate checkout. A path marked `gt-cloud:` is in gt-cloud, `gt:` marks one in the open-source gt repository and `content:` one in the content repository.

## Design vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rail | A rail is the single hairline on each side of the page column, drawn once by the column wrapper (`.tc-rail` with `border-inline: 1px solid var(--tc-hair)`). Since 2026-09-28 a column has one rail on each side, and the `single-rail` lint holds it. | [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) section 3; [`gt-landing-pages`](../../skills/gt-landing-pages/SKILL.md) "Rail and bands" |
| Band | A band is a horizontal section of a landing page. A band inside the column (`.tc-sec`) draws only its bottom rule, and a full-bleed band (`.tc-band.tcb`, `.v0-dep`) spans the viewport and draws its own rail pair once, at the column edges. | gt-landing-pages "Rail and bands" |
| Hatch spacer | A hatch spacer is a strip 28 to 40px tall with a hairline on each edge over a 45 degree hatch. It separates home bands (`.v0-hatch`) and rows inside a section (`.tc-hatch`), and it owns both of its edges, so the section before it drops its bottom rule. | gt-landing-pages "Hatch spacers"; DESIGN.md section 2 "Sanctioned devices" gives the shell's version, the book page's band (`.pt-book-band`), which also draws its own rule on both edges (2026-10-06) |
| Seam | A seam is the 1px line where two rows, cells or sections meet, formed by the row's hairline ground showing through a 1px gap. DESIGN.md section 10 also calls the slide-to-reveal handle the seam (`RevealSeam`, `--seam-cut`). | DESIGN.md sections 2 and 10 |
| The row owns every seam | This ownership rule for grids says that the row draws every structural line between its cells, cells draw no borders, and a framed cell shows the row's ground through a 1px padding reveal. Where a row meets a line that already exists, the cell sits flush and drops that side's reveal. | DESIGN.md section 2 "Ownership"; gt-landing-pages "The row owns every seam" |
| BentoRow and BentoCell | These two grid primitives implement that rule. The row is a grid with `gap-px` over the `--tc-hair` ground (props `cols`, `eqHeads` and `headH`), and the cell has no border props (`framed` adds the reveal). | `gt-cloud:apps/landing/src/components/landing/shell/Bento.tsx`, `src/components/shell/Bento.tsx`; gt-components |
| Registration cross | A registration cross is a small 1px plus seated exactly where two real hairlines cross. Kevin calls them corner crosses and DESIGN.md calls them border crosses, each landing section seam paints one at both rail corners, and a cross floating in app UI is ornament (2026-09-25). | DESIGN.md section 2 "Sanctioned devices"; gt-landing-pages "Registration crosses"; gt-aesthetic section 3 |
| The doubled line | The doubled line is GT's connector. One SVG path is stroked twice, a full ink stroke under a narrower stroke in the surface color, which leaves two parallel hairlines at a constant gap. It is the one sanctioned double line, also called the thread, and the GT mark draws every stroke in this grammar. | DESIGN.md section 5; `src/components/shared/diagrams/DoubledLine.tsx`; [`gt-diagrams`](../../skills/gt-diagrams/SKILL.md) |
| The plate | The plate is the dashboard's auth frame for the sign-in, consent, device, CLI and onboarding pages, with the form column on the left up to `--plate-edge` and the dithered field on the right. Elsewhere a plate is the one second surface under diagrams (`--pt-plate`), a layer of an isometric stack, or one of the speed marks. | `gt-cloud:apps/dashboard/src/components/frame/PlateFrame.tsx`, `src/components/brand/FieldStack.tsx` and `src/app/brand-tokens.css` there; the port in `src/components/plate/` |
| Hairline roles | The three line weights each have one job. The `hair` weight (ink at 18% in light) draws large surfaces and the lines that divide a page, `hair-soft` (ink at 9%) draws list rows, and `edge` (ink at 62%) frames a picture or a capture, so a large surface drawn in the edge weight reads as a boxed image. | DESIGN.md section 2 "Line law for chrome"; `src/components/viewer/tokens.css` |
| The book page | Every Prototemplate page with a book head renders one structure: the page's plain name as the title with nothing drawn above it, a lead of one to three lines, the panel (Updated, then the page's facts), one rule, an optional note and contents, one hatch band, then sections opened by a `Section n` divider. A page differs from another only in its words and its facts. | DESIGN.md section 4, The book page; `BookHead` in `src/components/viewer/BookView.tsx`; `pnpm lint:heads` |
| Rounded controls, square shells | Prototemplate's corner law: the shells that hold the interface (the frame, the sidebar, the toolbar bar, the sheet, rules and bands) are square, and everything a reader presses, types into or picks up is rounded: controls at 6px, a part inside one at 5px, chips at 4px, cards and popovers at 6px. | DESIGN.md section 2, Corners; the radius tokens in `src/components/viewer/tokens.css`; `pnpm lint:radius` |
| Ink | Ink is `#070707`, the darkest of the four absolute colors, used for content text on paper and as the ground of the dark theme. Raised ink (`#101010`) is the one dark artifact surface for code, config and diffs. | DESIGN.md section 1; `src/app/globals.css` |
| Ink-2 | Ink-2 is the second text step, for secondary text and leads (`--pt-ink-2`, `#3a3d44` in light). On a light surface, text a reader needs is set in ink-2 wherever titanium would fail contrast. | gt-aesthetic section 3; `src/components/viewer/tokens.css` |
| Titanium | Titanium is `#8a8f98`, the third and last text step, for captions, counters and keys. It measures 3.25:1 on white, so the dashboard plate darkens it to `#6e737c` for its 13px captions. | DESIGN.md section 1; gt-aesthetic section 3 |
| Paper | Paper is `#ffffff`, the light ground. Dark mode is a token remap in which paper collapses onto ink, so each theme has one surface. | DESIGN.md section 1 |
| GT blue | GT blue is the one accent, `#2f5ce0`, lifted to `#86a8ff` on dark bands. It marks actions, hover, highlights and icons while text and underlines stay ink, and a drawing gives it to one element. | DESIGN.md section 1; BRAND.md section 5; gt-aesthetic section 3 |
| The mobile type ladder | The mobile type ladder is the set of `--tcm-*` tokens that set type and spacing under 720px, declared in the engine's last 720px block and read as `var(--tcm-X, <px fallback>)`. The mobile cut in `v0-pages.css` outranks the engine on the `sgdh-root` pages, so a change to the ladder is made in both places. | DESIGN.md section 12 |
| The svh and dvh law | On a mobile stage, whatever must reach the true screen bottom sizes from `100dvh`, and every height that takes part in layout uses `100svh`. A layout height in dvh grows the document each time the URL bar shows or hides. | DESIGN.md section 13 |
| Read lines | The read lines are the two scroll anchors of a scroll story. A beat locks in when its copy block's center crosses the read line at 55% of the viewport, and its copy lights as it rises through the highlight line at 80%. | DESIGN.md section 14; `src/app/d/_v0/sections/FullStack.tsx` |
| The moving type law | The moving type law governs a word that morphs between languages. The word is one shaped text node with `lang` and `dir`, its width is measured from a hidden probe and is the only layout property that animates, and the host drives one clock per page. | DESIGN.md section 8; `src/components/shared/EverySentence.tsx`; [`gt-motion`](../../skills/gt-motion/SKILL.md) section 5 |
| Box-air | Box-air is the mobile standard for boxed cells: block padding of 36 and 38px (`--tcm-box-pt`, `--tcm-box-pb`), 28px (`--tcm-box-gap`) between a box's head and its artifact, and no copy within 20px of a hairline. | DESIGN.md section 12 "The box-air standard" |

## Working terms

| Term | Meaning | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lane | A lane is one owner's share of the work, with its own files: one session per lane, and inside a workflow one agent per lane. Work that belongs to another lane goes to that lane's session by name, and a lane's prompt opens with Kevin's own words for it. | [multi-session-playbook.md](multi-session-playbook.md) section 1; [`gt-orchestration`](../../skills/gt-orchestration/SKILL.md) section 2; gt-ship section 1; prototemplate section 8 |
| Session | A session is one Claude Code or Codex conversation. Kevin runs several named sessions at once (Videos, Prototemplate, New Onboarding and Dashboard, Glyph Map for `/world`), and each keeps to its lane. | multi-session-playbook.md section 1; prototemplate section 8 |
| Forked session | A forked session starts from another session's conversation and carries the parent's history, with the parent's session id on those records, before it continues on its own. A fork acts on its own lane, and a message found in several transcripts at the same minute can be one message inherited by forks. | `~/.claude/projects/<project>/<session>.jsonl`; multi-session-playbook.md section 2 |
| Worktree | A worktree is a git worktree with one branch, so each lane edits its own tree. Work in gt-cloud starts with `git worktree add -b k/<topic> <path> origin/main`, each worktree runs its own dev server port, and a worktree with its installs costs about 6 GB, so it is removed as soon as its work is pushed. | gt-ship section 1 |
| Chip | A chip is a background-task card that a session raises for an out-of-scope fix it noticed, and Kevin can spin it off into a new session. When he answers "Do this task here", the current session does the task as its own small PR with its own gates. | gt-orchestration section 3 |
| Handoff | A handoff is the document written at a hard stop or a change of owner, and it carries the stopped state, every raw input Kevin supplied and the priority queue. The receiving agent starts with an independent review and a recommendation, and it re-checks live state before changing anything. | gt-orchestration section 8 |
| Relay note | A relay note goes from one agent to another, often between Claude and Codex, and Kevin pastes it as is. It names the branch, tip commit and worktree, the commits, the receipts, the open list, the locks and the queued deploys, and the receiver finishes the other agent's open work. | multi-session-playbook.md section 7 |
| The ask ledger | The ask ledger is an itemized list of everything Kevin asked for in a run, including the sub-items of long messages and the add-ons sent mid-run, each with its status. It closes every report on multi-ask work, and a long run keeps it in a file so it survives compaction. | [`gt-reporting`](../../skills/gt-reporting/SKILL.md) section 3 |
| The PR slate | The PR slate answers "give me my PR list" with every open PR across gt-cloud, gt and content in merge order, in nine groups from merge now to the items only Kevin can do. Each line carries the full link, a one-line purpose and its line counts, and the Slack version is lowercase plain text in a code fence. | gt-reporting section 5, `scripts/pr-slate.mjs` |
| Explorations stay local | Exploration and redesign rounds stay uncommitted or on a branch until Kevin has reviewed them on localhost and says to land them, because Prototemplate main deploys the public site (2026-09-14). | gt-aesthetic section 5; gt-ship section 8; gt-explorations |
| Kevin merges | An agent merges a PR or runs `gh stack merge` only when Kevin says so in that session. | gt-ship sections 5 and 6 |
| The agent guide | `AGENTS.md` at the root of Prototemplate is the entry point for an agent: the read order, the principles in brief, which skill to load for a task, the house rules, and how to import the hub into another project. `CLAUDE.md` imports it for Claude Code. | `AGENTS.md`; `/docs/agents` |
